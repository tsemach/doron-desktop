Lawyers use several standard fee models depending on the type of case, client budget, and predictability of the work. While traditional hourly billing remains widespread, alternative fee arrangements (AFAs) have become increasingly common.

---

## Standard Legal Billing Models

### 1. Hourly Billing

The classic approach where attorneys charge a set rate for every hour (or fraction of an hour, typically in **6-minute increments**) spent working on a matter.

* **Best for:** Complex litigation, unpredictable corporate disputes, or ongoing advisory work.
* **Variations:**
* **Blended Rate:** A single hourly rate charged across the firm regardless of whether a partner, associate, or paralegal completes the task.
* **Capped Hourly Fee:** Hourly billing with a guaranteed maximum ceiling so costs don't spiral out of control.



### 2. Flat / Fixed Fees

A single predetermined price paid for a specific scope of work, regardless of how many hours it actually takes.

* **Best for:** Routine, predictable tasks like drafting wills, forming a corporation, simple real estate transactions, or standard trademark filings.

### 3. Contingency Fees

The attorney receives a percentage (typically **33% to 40%**) of the monetary recovery or settlement. If the client loses, the lawyer receives no legal fee (though the client may still be responsible for court expenses).

* **Best for:** Personal injury, medical malpractice, employment discrimination, and class actions.

### 4. Retainers (Advance Payment)

An upfront deposit paid into a trust account before work begins. As the lawyer performs work, they draw their earned fees out of this account.

* *Note:* Retainers are often paired with hourly billing—when the balance drops below a threshold, the client replenishes it.

## Alternative & Hybrid Models

| Billing Model | How It Works | Common Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Subscription / Flat Monthly** | A recurring monthly fee granting ongoing access to legal counsel for a fixed set of services. | Startups, small businesses, in-house counsel support. |
| **Unbundled / A La Carte** | Paying for specific discrete tasks (e.g., just reviewing a document) rather than full representation. | Self-represented litigants seeking targeted help. |
| **Hybrid / Partial Contingency** | A reduced hourly rate plus a smaller percentage of the eventual payout. | Business litigation where the client has some capital but wants shared risk. |
| **Success / Bonus Fees** | A base hourly or flat fee plus an extra bonus if a specific positive outcome is achieved. | M&A deals, high-stakes corporate disputes. |
| **Phased / Stage-Gated** | The project is broken into milestones, with separate fixed or estimated fees for each phase. | Large-scale litigation (e.g., discovery vs. trial). |
